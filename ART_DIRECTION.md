# Evidence in motion

The visual language is a research studio: warm paper, graphite type, generous margins and ember-orange annotations. The homepage uses the revised torus; `/sample-art` compares five forms inspired by https://asciihero.framer.website/ with original rendering code.

The ASCII studies describe mathematical surfaces through a small deterministic depth buffer. Light becomes character density, and selected points carry orange symbols. LaTeX fragments are embedded in the form rather than placed over reading content. Each variation explores a different relationship: continuity, intersecting fields, the page itself, waves and a shared thread.

The large form is cropped into the upper-right edge. Five static thumbnails support comparison while one preview moves at twenty frames per second. Surface-coordinate LaTeX anchors turn and disappear with the form; pointer movement gently steers its rotation. Movement stops offscreen, hidden, paused, or under reduced motion. No WebGL context, canvas framework or per-frame React state update is needed.

The flying paper appears on the homepage and gallery. It follows normal scrolling without pinning: a warm sheet twists into view behind black text, highlights words, and travels towards a diagram. Theme changes use a diagonal reveal with an immediate reduced-motion fallback. Both themes preserve the reading hierarchy and avoid blue surface tints.
